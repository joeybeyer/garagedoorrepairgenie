Repair Reality Check

  • Most garage door problems trace back to one of six parts: spring, cable, opener, sensor, roller, or panel.
  • A spring snap is the single most common failure — and the most dangerous to mishandle.
  • Most repairs finish in one visit when the tech arrives with a stocked van.
  • Service call fees are typically waived when you go forward with the repair.
  • Don't force a damaged door. One forced lift on a broken spring can wreck the panels and double your bill.

Before You Touch Anything

Garage doors are the heaviest moving object in most homes — a standard double door weighs 250 to 400 pounds, held up by springs under enough tension to throw a wrench across the room. The two rules that keep people safe:

  1. Do not force a door with a broken spring, snapped cable, or off-track section. The counterbalance is gone and the door can fall.
  2. Do not pull the manual release while standing under a closed door if the spring is broken. The door will drop fast and won't stop.

Park outside, keep kids and pets away from the garage, and let a tech handle the tensioned components.

What a Service Visit Actually Looks Like

  1. Call & dispatch. You describe the symptom; we identify the right tech and send them with the parts most likely needed. Usually same-day.
  2. On-site diagnosis. The tech arrives, tests the door manually, checks spring tension, inspects cables, runs the opener, and confirms the actual fault. This typically takes 10 to 15 minutes.
  3. Written estimate. Before any work happens, you get a flat-rate quote. If you decline, you owe only the service-call fee — which most homeowners get waived once they greenlight the repair.

Repair Questions People Actually Ask

How long does a typical repair take?

Most spring, cable, sensor, and remote-related fixes take 60 to 90 minutes from when the tech pulls up. A full opener replacement runs two to three hours. Panel replacement or full door replacement is a half-day or more.

Can I just use the door until you arrive?

If you heard a bang, see a gap in the spring, see a cable hanging slack, or the door is crooked — no. The counterbalance is gone and the door can fall. If the issue is sensor-related or remote-related, the door is generally safe to operate manually but we still recommend leaving it parked until a tech sees it.

Will you give me a flat-rate quote before working?

Yes. The tech inspects the door, identifies the parts needed, and writes the price before touching anything. You don't get surprised at the end.

How long will the repair last?

Spring replacements are typically rated for 10,000 to 20,000 cycles — about 7 to 15 years for a typical household. Opener gear replacements last roughly 5 to 10 years. Cables and rollers depend heavily on humidity and use.

Can the same tech also tune up the rest of the door while they're here?

Yes, and we recommend it. While the truck is parked in your driveway, a 15-minute lubrication, hinge tightening, and balance check usually adds little to the bill and prevents the next failure.

Do you work on commercial doors too?

Most of our network is residential-focused, but several markets have commercial-rated techs available. Mention your door size and door count when you call so the right person gets dispatched.
