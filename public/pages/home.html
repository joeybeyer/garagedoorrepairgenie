Common Problems We Solve

If any of these sound like your morning, stop forcing the door and call. Forcing a damaged garage door is how minor repairs become full assembly replacements.

The door will not open

Usually a broken spring, snapped cable, dead opener, or tripped breaker. Don't yank the manual release while you're standing under the door — if the spring is broken, the door can drop fast.

The door will not close

Most often a misaligned safety sensor, a blocked photo eye, or a limit-switch setting that drifted. Usually fixed in one visit.

A spring snapped

You'll hear a sharp bang from the garage. After that, the door feels suddenly heavy or won't lift at all. Don't try to lift it manually — spring replacement is a tech-only job.

Door stuck open

Now your house is wide open to the street. This is the most common emergency call we dispatch — we'll get it secured fast.

Door stuck closed

Car trapped, can't get to work. Often a cable jumped the drum or the opener gear stripped. We carry common parts for one-trip fixes.

A cable broke

The door usually drops crooked, sometimes wedging in the track. Don't run the opener after this happens — you'll bend the panels. More on cable repair.

Door came off track

Often caused by a bumper tap, a worn roller, or a snapped cable. The longer it sits twisted, the more likely it bends a section. See off-track repair.

Opener acts possessed

Closes a foot then reverses. Opens by itself at 2 a.m. Beeps without moving. Almost always sensor alignment, a stuck remote button, or a logic-board glitch.

Questions We Hear Every Week

How fast can someone actually get here?

Same-day in most service areas if you call before noon, often within two to four hours. Emergency calls (door stuck open, car trapped, business locked out) jump the queue.

Should I try to fix it myself first?

Some things, yes — replace the remote battery, wipe the photo-eye sensors with a clean cloth, check the wall outlet. Anything involving the spring, cable, or track tension is a hard no. Those parts are under high load and have hurt people.

Do you give pricing over the phone?

We give a realistic range based on the symptom you describe. Final numbers come after a tech sees the door, the spring count, the door weight, and the parts in stock. Anyone quoting an exact price sight-unseen is guessing.

What if my issue isn't on your service list?

Call anyway. The list covers the top 90% of calls but garage doors find creative ways to fail. If we can't help directly, we'll point you to someone who can.

Is the door safe to use right now?

If you heard a loud bang, see a gap in the spring above the door, see a cable hanging loose, or the door is leaning sideways — stop using it. The counterbalance system is compromised and the door can drop. Park outside and call.
