What Counts as a Garage Door Emergency

  • Door stuck open — house or business exposed, can't secure the property.
  • Door stuck closed — vehicle trapped, can't get to work or get home.
  • Door fell or is hanging dangerously — off-track, leaning, or panels separating.
  • Vehicle hit the door — bent panels, snapped cables, or damaged tracks.
  • Active break-in damage — door forced, hardware bent, security compromised.
  • Spring snap with door up — door is suspended without counterbalance and could drop.

Anything else — intermittent issues, slow doors, noisy travel, sensor blinks — gets scheduled at the next available slot, usually same-day or next-day.

Right Now, Before We Arrive

Garage doors weigh 250 to 400 pounds. When the counterbalance system fails, the door can fall fast and won't stop on its own. The actions you take in the next ten minutes determine whether this is a $300 fix or a $1,500 fix.

  • Don't try to lift a door with a broken spring or snapped cable. The door has no counterbalance and will fall.
  • Don't run the opener after a fall, off-track event, or vehicle impact. You'll bend more panels.
  • Don't drive under a partially-open door that's stuck or leaning. Park outside.
  • Don't let kids or pets near the door. Keep the area clear until a tech arrives.
  • If the door is stuck open and you're worried about security, call non-emergency police if you need a patrol while you wait.

What "Same-Day Emergency Dispatch" Actually Means

  1. Call lands. Dispatch identifies the emergency category, your location, and the most likely cause from your symptom description.
  2. Tech is rerouted. Active emergencies skip the regular schedule. The closest available tech with the right truck stock heads your way.
  3. ETA confirmed. You get a real time window — typically 60 to 180 minutes depending on traffic, location, and current load.
  4. Tech arrives, secures the door first if there's an active safety or security risk — closes the door, braces it, or bolts it down so it can't fall while the diagnosis happens.
  5. Quote, then repair. Even on emergency calls you get a flat-rate quote before work starts. No "we'll figure it out at the end" pricing.

After-hours and weekend dispatch typically carries a $50 to $150 premium over weekday-business-hours rates. That's how the on-call tech gets paid for showing up at 9 p.m.

How to Tell Dispatch What You Have

Three details make the trip faster and the truck stock more accurate:

  1. The symptom in plain language. "Door is stuck halfway up, won't go either way." "Heard a loud bang and now the door won't lift." "Backed into it this morning, panel is bent."
  2. Door size. Single-car (8 to 9 ft wide) vs. double-car (16 to 18 ft wide).
  3. Approximate door age if you know it — helps the tech bring the right replacement parts.

If you're not sure, just describe what you're seeing. The dispatcher will identify the rest.

Emergency Call Questions

What's the typical arrival time on an emergency call?

60 to 180 minutes in most service areas during business hours, longer at night or in heavy traffic. The dispatcher gives you a real ETA, not a "sometime today" window. If we can't get there fast enough, we'll tell you upfront so you can make other plans.

Is emergency service more expensive?

For weekday business hours, no — just same-day priority dispatch. For evenings, weekends, and holidays there's a typical $50 to $150 premium over standard rates. The repair work itself costs the same; the premium is for the on-call response.

What if I'm not home but the door is stuck?

We can usually still help. A neighbor, family member, or property manager with access can let the tech in. Some calls (broken cable, off-track door) can be diagnosed and quoted without entering, so we can prepare the parts before someone arrives to grant access.

My business is opening in an hour and the door is stuck. What now?

Call right away and tell dispatch you're a commercial site with a hard deadline. We can sometimes route an even-closer tech, and on rare occasions can guide a temporary safe-securing measure (lockout cones, temporary brace) so you can open while waiting for the full repair.

The door fell off the track and now I can't get my car out. Can the tech put it back?

Almost always, yes — but how long it takes depends on whether panels are bent, whether cables snapped, and whether the track itself is damaged. A clean off-track event with no panel damage takes 60 to 90 minutes. Bent panels or damaged tracks add time.
