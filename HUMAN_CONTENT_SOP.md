# Garage Door Repair Genie Human Content SOP

This adapts the "ultra-human" content method for a local garage door repair lead-gen site. The goal is not to trick readers or fabricate experience. The goal is to avoid flat, generic AI copy while keeping the content factual, useful, local, and conversion-focused.

## Core Rule

Write like a practical local service dispatcher explaining the issue to a homeowner on the phone.

Use:

- direct answers,
- short sentences where urgency matters,
- natural contractions,
- homeowner language,
- specific symptoms,
- local details,
- clear safety guidance,
- simple CTAs.

Avoid:

- fake personal anecdotes,
- fake technician stories,
- fake customer experiences,
- fake author credentials,
- forced slang,
- uncertainty around safety,
- over-casual jokes on emergency/safety pages,
- rambling introductions before the answer.

## Voice

Garage Door Repair Genie should sound:

- helpful,
- calm under pressure,
- lightly magical,
- safety-first,
- local without pretending to be physically at a specific address,
- direct enough to get the call.

Preferred phrases:

- "Call the Genie."
- "Do not force the door."
- "Garage door problem? Let's make it disappear."
- "The fastest move is to stop using the door and call."
- "A stuck-open door is a security issue."

Use contractions naturally:

- "don't" instead of "do not" in general advice,
- but keep "Do not force the door" as the safety phrase because it is clearer and stronger.

## Page Opening Formula

For PAA pages and answer pages:

1. H1 is the exact question.
2. First paragraph answers the question directly in 50-100 words.
3. Mention Garage Door Repair Genie once, naturally.
4. If safety-sensitive, include the safety action immediately.
5. No long story before the answer.

Example:

```text
If your garage door spring breaks, do not force the door open or keep running the opener. A broken spring means the door is no longer properly balanced and may be extremely heavy. Keep people clear of the door and call Garage Door Repair Genie for spring repair help.
```

## Humanization Without Fabrication

Use real-world specificity instead of fake anecdotes.

Good:

- "You may hear a loud bang from the garage."
- "The opener may hum while the door only moves a few inches."
- "A stuck-open garage leaves tools, bikes, and the entry door exposed."
- "In Portland, damp garages can accelerate cable corrosion."

Avoid:

- "Last Tuesday, I watched a homeowner..."
- "I remember fixing one in Buckhead..."
- "Our technician Mike says..." unless Mike is real and approved.

## Local Specificity

Make local content feel local by using verifiable details:

- neighborhoods from the page brief,
- climate patterns,
- housing stock,
- traffic or dispatch constraints,
- common garage conditions,
- nearby service areas.

Do not invent:

- exact response times,
- exact technician locations,
- fake addresses,
- fake jobs,
- fake local awards,
- fake review counts.

## Sentence Rhythm

Use varied sentence length.

Good pattern:

```text
Stop using the opener. If the cable snapped, the door can hang crooked or drop hard on one side. A technician needs to inspect the cable, spring tension, drums, rollers, and track before the door is safe to run again.
```

Avoid:

```text
Garage door cables are important components that play a vital role in the overall functionality and safety of your garage door system, and when they fail, it is important to contact a professional service provider.
```

## Headings

H2s should sound like sections a homeowner would scan.

Good:

- "Signs the Spring Broke"
- "Why You Should Not Force the Door"
- "What to Do While You Wait"
- "When to Call the Genie"

Avoid:

- "Comprehensive Garage Door Spring Repair Optimization Overview"
- repeated exact-match keywords in every H2,
- vague headings like "Important Information."

## Safety Copy

Never soften safety language to sound more conversational.

Required safety phrase for spring, cable, off-track, and emergency topics:

```text
Do not force a garage door with a broken spring, snapped cable, or off-track section. Garage doors are heavy and can be dangerous when the counterbalance system fails.
```

Acceptable variations can be used nearby, but this core meaning must remain.

## CTA Style

CTAs should be short and practical.

Good:

- "Broken spring? Do not force the door. Call Garage Door Repair Genie."
- "Door stuck open? Call now so the opening can be secured."
- "Not sure what's wrong? Call the Genie and describe what the door is doing."

Avoid:

- "Unlock a seamless garage door experience today."
- "Embark on your garage repair journey."
- "We are committed to providing unparalleled excellence."

## Editing Passes

Use this pass sequence before publishing or regenerating pages.

1. Answer pass: Is the answer in the first 50-100 words?
2. Safety pass: Is the unsafe action clearly stopped?
3. Specificity pass: Are there concrete symptoms, parts, or local details?
4. Plain-English pass: Remove bloated phrasing.
5. Rhythm pass: Mix short and medium sentences.
6. CTA pass: Is the phone call the obvious next step?
7. Schema pass: Does FAQPage schema match visible answers?
8. Link pass: Does the page link to the right money page?

## AI Review Prompt

Use this prompt to critique a draft:

```text
Review this garage door repair page. Does it sound generic, AI-written, or over-polished? Identify the top 5 phrases that feel artificial. Then rewrite only those phrases so they sound like a calm local service dispatcher explaining the issue to a homeowner. Do not invent personal stories, credentials, customer examples, exact pricing, or response times.
```

## Final Checklist

- The first paragraph answers the question.
- The page does not use fake anecdotes.
- The page includes specific symptoms or causes.
- Safety language is clear.
- The CTA is visible and direct.
- The tone is helpful, not theatrical.
- The page links to the correct service page.
- FAQ schema matches on-page answers.
- Local references are real and relevant.
- No exact-match keyword stuffing in H2s.
